\title{A reproducible workflow for LLM-proposed mathematical results:
Erd\H{o}s-template prompts, adversarial verification subagents,
automated literature diff, and accumulating calibration}

\begin{abstract}
Frontier LLMs (ChatGPT-Pro, Claude Opus 4.7, etc.) can propose
non-trivial mathematical results---closed forms, identity
verifications, structural arguments---at a rate substantially higher
than any individual researcher. The bottleneck is no longer
\emph{generation} but \emph{filtering}: separating genuine novel
contributions from rediscoveries of classical material, from
plausible-looking but incorrect derivations, and from
calibrated-honest ``I don't know'' responses that the model already
correctly produces. We document a four-component workflow developed
during a 30-cycle private research run (Ramanujan-mining project,
2026-05) that filters LLM proposals reliably enough to produce
publication-grade output: (1)~an Erd\H{o}s-style prompt template with
open T/F formulation, machinery loading, anti-handwave guards, and
escape hatches; (2)~domain-specific machinery loading that
accumulates context across cycles; (3)~a verification gate of
adversarial subagents (independent reimplementation $+$ numerical
verification $+$ literature diff); and (4)~diary-discipline $+$ a
``did not pan out'' log that compounds calibration over time. We
report the cycle-by-cycle hit rate: of approximately $30$ substantive
ChatGPT-Pro cycles, $\sim 7$ produced calibrated-honest negatives
that the model correctly flagged (``I do not have a closed form for
this''), $\sim 16$ produced confirmed-known content (useful as
implementations, not as novel-math claims), and $1$ produced a
genuinely novel mathematical finding (a correction to a published
Andrews--Berndt commentary on Ramanujan's \emph{Lost Notebook}
Entry~4.2.3). The methodology's strongest single output is the
\emph{verification-gate pattern}: $4$ of $4$ adversarial subagents
returned actionable verdicts that either confirmed, partially
confirmed, or downgraded a prior LLM claim; in each case the verdict
was the one that survived in the final paper output. We release the
full workflow, prompt templates, verification scripts, and diary
record at \url{https://github.com/jounih/ramanujan-mining} for
reproduction and critique.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

A frontier LLM can be a remarkably productive mathematical
collaborator. Given a well-posed problem in a specific subfield, it
can frequently propose a closed-form expression, an identity, or a
structural argument that would take a human researcher hours to
construct from scratch. But the same LLM, given the same problem,
will sometimes also propose a plausible-looking but incorrect answer;
or rediscover a classical result without flagging the prior art; or
overclaim ``novel'' status for something derivable in five lines of
textbook calculation; or---and this is the most useful case---honestly
report that it cannot solve the problem and identify the specific
obstruction.

The bottleneck for productive use of LLM mathematical proposers is
therefore not generation but filtering. This paper reports a workflow
we developed during a private 30-cycle research run (Ramanujan-mining,
May 2026) that filters LLM proposals reliably enough to produce
publication-grade output: one short corrigendum (Andrews--Berndt
\emph{Lost Notebook} Part~IV, Entry~4.2.3) and five verified
computational primitives covering ML attention, numerical PDEs,
port-Hamiltonian simulation, asymptotic special-function evaluation,
and Madelung lattice sums.

We argue that the workflow generalizes beyond Ramanujan-style work,
and we document it here for reproduction.

\section{The four-component workflow}\label{sec:workflow}

\subsection{Erd\H{o}s-style prompt template}\label{sec:prompt-template}

Every productive prompt in the run followed roughly the same skeleton,
modeled after OpenAI's chain-of-thought demonstrations on
Erd\H{o}s-style problems~\cite{OpenAIErdos2026}:

\begin{quote}
\begin{verbatim}
CONTEXT. [1-3 paragraphs loading the specific machinery: operator
basis, recent verified results, catalogues to check against,
recent gotchas]

OPEN QUESTION. [The actual T/F or construct-or-prove-impossible
deliverable]

PRIORS / GUARDRAILS. [What most experts expect; what the failure
modes look like; specific things to avoid]

DELIVERABLE FORMAT. [Concrete output schema -- explicit LaTeX +
numerical check at N digits + cost ratio + provenance]

IF YOU CAN'T. [Escape hatch -- "tell me the obstruction" -- turns
failure into information]
\end{verbatim}
\end{quote}

Four design choices in this template matter:

\paragraph{Open T/F formulation.} ``Does $X$ hold, or doesn't it?''
rather than ``Prove $X$'' or ``Disprove $X$''. The first lets the
model pick the productive direction; the second two bias toward one
answer.

\paragraph{Specific numerical bar.} ``$\ge 30$ digits agreement,''
``$\le 10^{-100}$ relative error,'' ``$\ge 3\times$ wall-clock
speedup.'' Vague verification gates produce vague answers.

\paragraph{Anti-handwave clause.} ``\,`It's likely hard' is not an
answer.'' Pushes the model to either commit to a construction or
commit to a precise structural argument.

\paragraph{Escape hatch.} ``If you can't, tell me the obstruction.''
Converts failure into information. The $7$ calibrated-honest cycles
in our run (see \cref{sec:honest-cycles}) all followed this
pattern---the model returned a clean ``no'' with the specific
structural obstruction identified, rather than a fabricated answer.

\subsection{Domain-specific machinery loading (the leverage compound)}\label{sec:machinery}

Every prompt loads the relevant context as prefix to the actual
question. For our Ramanujan-flavor prompts, this meant:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
\item The 12-operator basis with one-line descriptions.
\item Recent verified results (so the model knows what's already
done).
\item The toolkit-split prior (which operator clusters fire on which
problem types).
\item Specific catalogues to cross-check against (Berndt's
notebooks~\cite{BerndtNB}, Andrews--Berndt \emph{Lost
Notebook}~\cite{AB2013}, OEIS, arXiv).
\item Recent gotchas from prior cycles (e.g., pre-declare
low-complexity alpha-class to avoid tautological-fit).
\end{itemize}

Each new prompt incorporates what prior prompts learned. After $30$
cycles, the prompts are loaded with accumulated calibration. The
\emph{hit rate climbs over time}---not because the model is smarter,
but because the prompts are better-targeted.

\subsection{The verification-gate pattern (adversarial subagents)}\label{sec:verification-gate}

This is the most important component. \emph{Every numerical claim
from the LLM proposer must pass independent verification.} We
implemented this with adversarial subagents---distinct Claude /
GPT-4 agent instances, each given the original LLM claim and asked
to \emph{actively try to break it}, not rubber-stamp it.

The subagent pattern in our run:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item LLM proposer produces a claim with numerical evidence at $N$
digits.
\item Independent subagent reimplements the construction from
scratch.
\item Subagent verifies at $N+$ digits across an expanded sweep of
cells (orders of magnitude, edge cases, structural extensions).
\item Subagent does explicit literature diff (local PDF grep + arXiv
WebFetch + Google Scholar).
\item Subagent returns: CONFIRMED / PARTIAL / REFUTED / DOWNGRADED.
\item The verdict is the citable record, not the original LLM claim.
\end{enumerate}

Three subagents in our run returned verdicts that \emph{downgraded}
an original LLM claim:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
\item \textbf{Level-$pq$ Lambert annihilator family} (diary~55
$\to$~58): math CONFIRMED at $25/25$ cells $\times$ dps=$200$, but
\emph{novelty downgraded} from ``infinite family of novel identities,
$9.5/10$, Trans.\ AMS, $6$--$12$ pages'' to ``engine autonomously
rediscovers classical $\dim E_2(\Gamma_0(pq)) = 3$ with elegant gcd
parameterization, $4.5/10$, Math.\ Mag.\ or Amer.\ Math.\ Monthly
expository note, $2$--$4$ pages'' after the literature diff revealed
the structure is classical.

\item \textbf{PH spring-mass Bessel-image propagator speedup}
(diary~53 $\to$~59): math CONFIRMED at machine epsilon, but
\emph{speedup magnitude recalibrated} from ``$95\times$ vs DFT''
(which turned out to be vs mpmath-DPS DFT) to ``$1.7\times$ vs
numpy-FFT~/ $5{,}762\times$ vs Verlet''---the same construction
yields different speedup numbers against different baselines.

\item \textbf{$4$ cross-domain accelerators prior-art audit}
(diary~60): all $4$ implementations CONFIRMED, but $3$ of $4$ were
attributed to prior literature (RetNet/Mamba-SSD for finite-pole
attention, Crowdy--Marshall for annular Green, Schr\"odinger~1914
$+$ Trovato~2016 for PH Bessel-image), with one (Airy Tricomi-$U$ at
$(1/6, 1/3)$ for $\mathrm{Ai}(-x)$) flagged as ``DLMF-adjacent with
parameters less-documented in canonical references.''
\end{itemize}

One subagent CONFIRMED an original claim at high confidence:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
\item \textbf{AB Entry~4.2.3 defense} (diary~56 $\to$~57): $5$
independent verification paths converge ($223/223$ cells at
dps=$80$; closed form at $\lambda=1/2$, $\varepsilon=1$ reduces
both sides to $e^a \cdot \operatorname{erf}(\sqrt{a})$ exactly;
independent Mittag-Leffler contour-integral proof via
Bromwich/Hankel; literature audit found no published treatment
since AB~2013---$12$ years unchallenged). \emph{Recommended output:
Ramanujan Journal corrigendum, $3$--$5$ pages, with courtesy
heads-up to Bruce Berndt before submission.}
\end{itemize}

This is the only result in the entire $30$-cycle run that survives
the verification gate as a \emph{novel mathematical contribution}.
Everything else is either a verified implementation of known math,
a calibrated-honest negative, or a downgraded packaging contribution.

\subsection{Diary discipline $+$ accumulating calibration}\label{sec:diary}

Every cycle (productive or unproductive) produces a numbered diary
entry under \texttt{diary/NN\_topic.md}. The diary records:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
\item The original LLM claim.
\item The verification verdict.
\item The literature audit.
\item Cross-links to prior cycles.
\item Lessons captured (which get folded into subsequent prompts).
\end{itemize}

A parallel \texttt{DID\_NOT\_PAN\_OUT.md} log records every refuted~/
corrected~/ downgraded speculation. In our run, this file has $19$
entries---substantial enough to constitute a methodological
contribution in its own right. \emph{The credibility of the run
depends on this file growing alongside the positive-finding log.}

The result is a workflow where every cycle's lessons compound. The
hit rate climbs not because the model gets smarter but because the
prompts and the verification gate get better-targeted.

\section{What current LLMs are actually contributing}\label{sec:contributions}

This is worth saying explicitly. In our run:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
\item \emph{The math came from ChatGPT-Pro.} Derivations, closed
forms, asymptotic analyses, structural arguments---all proposed by
ChatGPT-Pro and (when correct) verified independently.
\item \emph{The methodology came from us.} Prompt design, machinery
loading, verification orchestration, diary discipline, literature
diff, prior-art attribution.
\item \emph{The verification subagents are also LLM-based.} Claude
Opus~4.7 agents do the adversarial reimplementation. This is not
circular---the proposer and the verifier are independent agent
instances, each unaware of the other's reasoning trace.
\end{itemize}

Without ChatGPT-Pro, the methodology produces nothing. Without the
methodology, ChatGPT-Pro produces a mix of correct, incorrect, and
incidentally-novel proposals indistinguishably. \emph{The combination
is what filters reliably.}

\section{What this implies for math-research workflow}\label{sec:implications}

We extract three implications:

\paragraph{(a) Frontier LLMs should be used as proposers, not as
authorities.}
The proposer--verifier--gate--store pattern is robust; the ``ask the
LLM and trust the answer'' pattern is not.

\paragraph{(b) Verification gates should be adversarial, not
collaborative.}
A subagent told to ``verify this claim'' will rubber-stamp; one told
to ``actively try to break this claim, then report what survives''
will catch real errors. In our run, three of four adversarial
subagents returned downgrades---that's a $75\%$ calibration miss
rate in the proposer's original claims that would have shipped
silently under a less-adversarial regime.

\paragraph{(c) The literature-diff step is non-optional.}
Three of four cross-domain accelerators in our run turned out to
have direct prior publications. If we had skipped the prior-art
audit, we would have shipped $4$ ``novel'' claims that were actually
$3$ known $+$ $1$ partial. A computational tool paper that
attributes its constructions correctly is still publishable; one
that overclaims novelty is not.

\section{Reproducibility}\label{sec:reproducibility}

The full methodology---prompt templates, verification subagent
prompts, diary entries, ``did not pan out'' log, paper drafts, code,
per-cell CSVs---is publicly accessible at~\cite{ProjectC}.

The Erd\H{o}s-style prompt template at
\verb|insights/CHATGPT_PRO_PROMPT_DESIGN_METHODOLOGY.md| is reusable
for any subfield with three properties: (i)~a small composable
operator basis exists; (ii)~numerical verification is cheap (e.g.,
\texttt{mpmath} at $30+$ digits is fast for the problem class);
(iii)~a literature corpus is locally accessible for prior-art audit.

\paragraph{Expected hit rate for a researcher with ChatGPT-Pro
access running this workflow on a comparable corpus:}
${\sim}5$--$10\%$ novel-math yield ($1$ of $\sim 30$ cycles in our
run), ${\sim}50$--$70\%$ useful-known-math yield, ${\sim}20$--$30\%$
calibrated-honest negative yield. The novel-math yield in particular
depends heavily on whether the targeted subfield has any open
questions that fall within the LLM's current capability envelope.

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{The $1$-in-$30$ novel-math rate may not generalize.}
Our subfield (Ramanujan-style $q$-series, theta functions, Lambert
identities) has a substantial open-question landscape that maps well
to current LLM capabilities. Other subfields may have higher or
lower yield.

\paragraph{The verification subagents are themselves LLM-based and
could share blind spots with the proposer.}
We mitigate this with explicit numerical bars (\texttt{mpmath} at
$30+$ digits is hard to fake) and multiple independent verification
paths (in the AB~4.2.3 case: $5$ distinct paths, including a
\texttt{sympy}-symbolic reduction that is a non-neural component).

\paragraph{The literature-diff step is incomplete.}
WebFetch $+$ arXiv $+$ Google Scholar miss paywalled~/ non-indexed~/
older~/ non-English material. For our run, this caught only the
obvious prior-art cases; deeper checks against textbook material
(e.g., Olver's \emph{Asymptotics and Special Functions} for Airy
Tricomi-$U$) require offline access we did not have.

\paragraph{The ``did not pan out'' log is a credibility floor, not a
ceiling.}
A research run that publishes its $19$ refuted/downgraded
speculations alongside its $1$ surviving novel finding is more
trustworthy than one that publishes only the wins. But the
trustworthiness comes from external scrutiny of the log, not from
the log itself.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

We have documented a four-component workflow---Erd\H{o}s-style
prompts, machinery loading, adversarial verification subagents,
diary discipline---that filters LLM mathematical proposals reliably
enough to produce publication-grade output. In a $30$-cycle private
research run, the workflow produced $1$ novel mathematical finding
(an AB \emph{Lost Notebook} corrigendum), $5$ verified computational
primitives (cross-domain accelerators), $1$ elegant rediscovery of
classical structure, and $7$ calibrated-honest negatives (where the
model correctly identified the obstruction). The single most
consequential output is the corrigendum; the most important
methodological finding is the $75\%$ downgrade rate of the
adversarial verification subagents, which says that the unverified
proposer rate of overclaim is high enough that the verification gate
is essential.

We release the full workflow at~\cite{ProjectC} for reproduction and
critique.
